Learning human 3D Gaussian Splatting from sparse 2D views.

Abstract

Existing human novel view synthesis (NVS) methods typically rely on textured 3D scans, dense pose-aligned multiview images, or parametric body priors such as SMPL, making their training data costly to acquire and difficult to scale beyond controlled capture settings. We present SPINS, a framework for single-image human NVS that learns feed-forward human 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) from sparse 2D view supervision. Given a single input image, a human-specific multiview diffusion module first synthesizes a small set of anatomically plausible canonical views. An object-centric 3DGS reconstruction model, initialized from large-scale pretraining, is then adapted with only sparse 2D human views to convert the synthesized views into a 3DGS representation. We introduce a sparsely supervised perturb-and-reconstruct objective: Gaussians predicted from the synthesized views are rendered at offset viewpoints, and the reconstruction obtained from those renderings must reproduce the original views. Geometric errors that are invisible from the supervised views become visible once the asset is rendered from elsewhere, and the round trip converts them into reconstruction error that the model is driven to correct. The objective, therefore, enforces stable geometry and appearance across viewpoints with nothing beyond the sparse 2D views as supervision. Trained once on four views per subject of a single dataset, SPINS remains competitive with densely supervised methods across three benchmarks despite using no dense multiview, textured-mesh, or SMPL supervision, and is markedly more resilient to partial occlusions of the input view. These results show that single-image human 3DGS can be learned without dense multiview capture, textured meshes, or explicit parametric body priors.

Method overview

Generate a small set of canonical views, reconstruct a 3D human, and learn from what changes when the viewpoint moves.

From one image to 3D. A human-specific multiview diffusion module synthesizes anatomically plausible canonical views. A pretrained object-centric reconstruction model, adapted with sparse human views, converts them into a 3D Gaussian representation.

Perturb and reconstruct. The predicted Gaussians are rendered from offset viewpoints and reconstructed again. The second reconstruction must reproduce the original views, exposing geometric errors that are difficult to observe from the supervised viewpoints alone.

Progressive viewpoint offsets. Viewpoint perturbations grow during adaptation, gradually asking the model to maintain geometry and appearance across a wider range of viewpoints.
